# E1 pilot — a rehearsal, and the first paired variance read

**Date:** 2026-10-01
**Instrument:** `pi-wiki-eval` + the A/B harness (`eval/`), all self-checks green (29 harness checks,
3 of 3 graders verified to fail on the untouched base)
**Status:** complete — a rehearsal for the pipeline, not evidence about the wiki
**Plan:** `docs/plans/EFFICACY.md` §11 (E1)

## 1. Purpose

Three things at once: prove the harness works end to end on a real repository, measure the **paired SD**
that sizes the real experiment, and get a first look at where a wiki-armed agent's budget actually goes.
It was never powered to answer "does the wiki help", and with three tasks it could not be.

## 4. Integrity — the part that makes the numbers usable

The harness's own guarantees held on every run: **0 timeouts, 0 arm-verification mismatches** (each arm
loaded exactly the tool set it should), **0 shared-dependency changes**, no target commit reachable, and
all 9 runs graded. The fairness line reported the arms as comparable — 10 shared non-wiki tools, 15 wiki
tools in the treatment only — which is the check that caught the earlier defect where the control arm
was silently running without any extensions at all.

## 5. What the pilot actually shows

**1. These tasks were too easy to discriminate.** Every arm passed every task, so the outcome guardrail
carried no information: the only thing left to compare was cost. A task where the control arm always
succeeds cannot show that the wiki prevented a mistake — it can only show overhead. R1 needs tasks where
the `off` arm sometimes fails.

**2. The wiki's cost premium is maintenance, not consultation.** Every wiki-arm run made 5–15 wiki
*calls* and only 2–3 wiki *reads*. The calls are upkeep — capture, review, finalize at settle — which
happens on every task whether or not the wiki is ever read. So "having the wiki installed" carries a
standing cost per task, and any efficacy claim has to price that in. This is precisely the distinction
the read/write split was added for, and it is the single most useful number the pilot produced.

**3. Pairing collapses the variance.** The paired SD is 0.24× the off-arm mean, against a between-episode
coefficient of variation near 1.1 in the R0 observational data. That is what makes a controlled
experiment affordable at all: on this figure a 20% effect needs about 12 task-instances, where the
unpaired comparison needed hundreds. It rests on three pairs, so treat it as an upper bound on
optimism and re-measure it in R1.

**4. The observed +24% is inside the noise.** With three pairs the design detects roughly a 38% effect;
+24% is not distinguishable from zero, and the sign should not be read as "the wiki costs more". The
honest statement is that this pilot cannot tell.

**5. Rediscovery is measurable but rarely.** Only one of nine runs retrieved a page that declares
`files:` links, and it scored 52%. The other eight had no retrieval at all, or none against pages that
declare files — the same limitation R0 found, now visible in a controlled setting.

**6. The timeout is too small for heavier tasks.** ds-003 used 247–250s of its 300s budget. A slightly
harder task would have been killed and recorded as a timeout; raise `timeoutMinutes` to 10 before R1.

**7. Why the brief arm matters, and the hypothesis it suggests.** The `brief` arm was **cheapest on the
hardest task** (ds-003: $0.0655 against the control's $0.0822, −20%) and more expensive on the easiest one
(ds-002: $0.0460 against $0.0373). That is the shape you would expect if a little structured knowledge
saves a lot of searching precisely when the task is hard enough to require it — and the wiki proper pays
for the same knowledge plus its upkeep, which is where its premium went. **This is a hypothesis, not a
finding**: three tasks, one repetition, and differences of this size sit inside the noise band for n=3
(§5.4). But it is the reason to attack maintenance cost specifically, and it predicts an interaction that
R1 can test directly: the knowledge benefit should grow with task difficulty while the upkeep cost stays
flat, and the `wiki-nocapture` arm is what separates the two.

## 6. Changes before R1

1. **Harder tasks** chosen so the `off` arm fails sometimes — a task every arm passes measures nothing
   but overhead.
2. **Timeout 10 minutes**, on the evidence of ds-003.
3. **Consider a fourth arm: wiki present, capture off** (`wiki-nocapture`, now implemented). It separates
   "the wiki's upkeep costs this much" from "the wiki's retrieval helps this much" — the two are currently
   entangled in one number, and upkeep is where the pilot's premium went.
4. **Judge quality, not just pass/fail.** Every run now preserves its diff under `diffs/` and collects the
   project's own type-checker and linter over what the arm wrote; `eval/judge.mjs` sends each task's diffs
   to a blinded subagent that scores six quality dimensions and flags a diff that weakened the tests it was
   graded by. The pilot above predates this, so its arms can no longer be reviewed — the first judged run
   will be the next one.
4. **More task-instances**, sized from a re-measured paired SD with ≥12 pairs rather than the optimistic
   three-pair estimate.
5. **Replay cards as soon as the repository has real work commits**, so the grader is the project's own
   tests and there is a ground-truth diff, instead of a contract I authored.

## 7. Limitations

- Three tasks, one model, one repository, one repetition. Nothing here generalises.
- Forward tasks with `target: null`: no ground-truth diff, and the graders encode a contract rather than
  the project's own tests.
- The graders cover the decision logic and the owner check; the Discord gateway wiring in ds-002 and the
  slash-command registration in ds-003 were **not** graded (they need a live connection) and are
  reviewed by hand.
- Costs are provider-reported and model-specific; they will not transfer to another model.

## 8. Reproduction

```bash
cd C:/Coding/pi-jev-wiki
node eval/selftest.mjs                                    # harness self-checks, no cost
node eval/grade-check.mjs --tasks eval/tasks/ds-001.json,eval/tasks/ds-002.json,eval/tasks/ds-003.json
node eval/run.mjs --tasks eval/tasks/ds-001.json,eval/tasks/ds-002.json,eval/tasks/ds-003.json \
     --arms off,brief,wiki --repeats 1 --model deepseek-flash --runs-dir eval/runs
node eval/report.mjs --run <run-id>
```

Per-run records, sessions and logs are under `eval/runs/<run-id>/` (gitignored). This document is the
durable record of that run.
